Making mass conserving Cellular Automata and their categorization.

that falling sand gif

Mass conservation here means that each state of a CA has associated mass number, non negative integer, such that all patterns conserve the sum of those numbers under application of generations. And vacuum is assumed to have 0 mass.

Population conserving / Number conserving CAs are the terms used in literature often, mostly about 2 state such CAs.

Many terms used here can be found on wiki or https://conwaylife.com/

Overview of the well known models

  • Lattice Gas Cellular Automata (LGCA) e.g. HPP / FHP, gas simulation CAs, reversible quasi-isotropic, HPP can be realized as 2^4 state vN CA. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPP_model

  • Falling sand simulation “CAs”, usually not CAs per se as they lack simultaneous update. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VLZjd_Y1gJ8 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4u7Zy_b868

  • Margolus neighborhood CAs, can be reversible, but necessarily quasi-isotropic or anisotropic, realized as 2^4 state Moore CA. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_cellular_automaton https://people.csail.mit.edu/nhm/cam-book.pdf

  • Kenichi Morita’s partitioned CAs, some are reversible, all quasi-isotropic or anisotropic. Close to Margolus, but with native vN neighborhood, 2^4 states. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10710-025-09521-w

  • Abelian sandpile model, isotropic, but no oscillators / ships are possible as it invariably diffuses and settles. The interest comes primarily from looking at ever higher state numbers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_sandpile_model

  • Biham–Middleton–Levine traffic model, anisotropic, can be realized naively by 6 state vN CA. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biham–Middleton–Levine_traffic_model

  • Nagel–Schreckenberg model similar to previous, but 1d, with larger neighborhood and randomization. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagel%E2%80%93Schreckenberg_model

  • Biological lattice-gas cellular automaton (BIO-LGCA), variations of FHP, mixed with random transitions usually. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIO-LGCA

  • Q2R Ising CA 2 state CA with “energy” associated with patterns rather than singular cell states. Each cell is either - or +, and it flips each generation if it has the same number of vN neighbors with + and with -, otherwise stays the same. Updates alternate like checkerboard, black-white-black-…. Energy is computed by counting adjacent ++ / – pairs as +1 and -+ pairs as -1 and summing them, and it is conserved. Arbitrary structures can be made out of cells gained by ripping 0 into +1-1 and separating them, so it’s not mass conserving in spirit. Also translation invariance is somewhat broken. https://scispace.com/pdf/periods-and-clusters-in-ising-cellular-automata-48gwor011h.pdf

General sources:

  • 1d 2 state Number conserving CAs, E.g. Rule 184 a lot of literature on them, mostly boring in ways it approaches exploring them. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00060 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_184

  • n-d 2 state Number conserving CAs, a lot of literature on them in academia, but they go about it in a boring way.

General qualities as applied to MC CAs

Isotropy means the rule is exactly D4-symmetric if it’s on the square lattice, given transition function F and some combination of rotations and reflection R, then R F R^-1 = F. For example CGOL is fully isotropic. MAP rules are general form, containing many anisotropic rules, for example a rule where everything moves to the left each generation. Other lattices or higher dimensions have different symmetry groups, isotropy there means rule is invariant under that.

Partial case here is quasi-isotropy, when it’s the case that S R F R^-1 S^-1 = F, S being permutation of states corresponding to this R, such as for example permutation changing the arrow direction of the state. HPP, Margolus, Morita’s PCAs are examples or at least contain some such rules.

This is a genuine relaxation, if you allow altering the states of the CA in symmetry, it gives so much additional different symmetries, like Day and Night rule where S F S ^-1 = F, S is a state flip. There are also rules that commute F G = G F, and are reversible, so one rule is a “symmetry” for another and vice versa.

Other assumed properties here are:

Simultaneous update, deterministic, finite neighborhood, discrete state of cells, finite number of states of cells, square lattice, translation invariance.

In this writeup, it is assumed that if the update function has two stages / parts, they should be considered as joined into one, in the end. So there should be no partial updates, no 3.5 generations. Staged updates are just an abstraction. E.g. Margolus neighborhood CAs are ultimately 16 state Moore CAs.

Also cell-centered square lattice, so that the squares have the states, not e.g. edges on it, like in the most basic HPP formulation.

Mass conserving CA can be reversible, but there is no strong coupling of those properties. In many rule spaces, reversible by construction CAs are very explosive. And mass conserving are losing information very quickly, for example CA where all vertical 10 dominoes swap, with state 2 being stable “wall” – if you imagine making random filled square over horizontal wall, then mass falls down like sand and piles up, losing spacing information in columns. This is common.

Speed of light = c. Should be noted that orthogonal speed of light in Moore and diagonal speed are not the same per se. When doing an orthogonal jump you can do another orthogonal jump perpendicular to the first. That gives rise to ships going at c where slices perpendicular to the direction of motion can conduct signals up and down.

Both diagonal Moore c-ships and all vN c-ships cannot, for any diagonals. But they are not completely static in the sense they can be of arbitrarily high period, e.g. by implementing something like ripple counters with leading cells providing the clock signal. (pointed out by Magma)

It’s hard to define what is that is conserved when you deal with infinite patterns, so here is mostly used the definition that applies only to finite patterns on vacuum background, or toruses, or finite regions with immutable wall circling them.

As an example of infinite pattern weirdness, imagine a beam of 1 mass cells, that has a start but extends to infinity. If the starting cell dies, did the mass change?

Let’s say you prohibit the case above by saying it’s mass conserving only if sum of decrease in mass of all cells is equal to the sum of increase, each generation. But then what happens if both sums are infinite?

Promising way to handle this is to require the existence of a bijection that connects all disappearing mass units and all appearing mass units one to one, with constraint that the distance of all such connections is bounded, on all patterns. – prohibits infinite beam with dying starting cell, allows infinite horizontal beam to move one cell up, prohibits beam’s cells with indexes 2^n dying and all cells 1 step below the beam becoming alive.

GPT5.5: We say that FF is mass-conserving if there exists a constant B<∞B < \infty such that, for every configuration QQ, there is a bijection ϕQ:DQ→AQ\phi_Q : D_Q \to A_Q from disappearing mass units to appearing mass units satisfying distance(x,ϕQ(x))≤B for all x∈DQ.distance\bigl(x,\phi_Q(x)\bigr) \le B \text{ for all } x \in D_Q.

Unclear if this is a good definition for CAs on infinite patterns. Also even HPP fails this criterion above, as you can make infinite beam with first cell emptying out in it, so maybe it’s not very well applicable criterion. But dealing with this stuff in general is not in the scope here, finite patterns are the focus.

Properties of MC CA rules, decided by design rather than emergent

SWAP01 = vN rule with 2 states, for all horizontal 01 dominoes, swap them. Can be realized into actual transition table, but this is a better way to state it.

What is the state of the cell?

  • Transitions depend only on the mass number of the central cell, and mass numbers of its neighbors.
  • Non zero mass cells can hold additional state info e.g. mass 2 type 1 and mass 2 type 2 cells.
  • Also zero mass cells can hold state, e.g. mass 0 type 1 and mass 0 type 2 cells
  • If massless non vacuum cell states are there. Can there be finite cell configurations that increase amount of massless states as a proportion to massed states arbitrarily high as generations are applied to it? Yes e.g. non interacting second layer of CGOL defined to be massless / No e.g. vacuum transitions to 0 mass state if nearby of 1 mass state, but otherwise does nothing and goes back to normal vacuum if that condition stops.

Many quasi-isotropic CAs have state of the cell that holds state beyond the mass number of the cell, for example HPP, 2-mass cell can be the one with arrows at 180 or 90 degrees.

Adding stateful vacuum is slightly weird move in this context, as it can become the case that all the interesting behaviors happen there, so you essentially made non-MC CA.

But it can be interesting nonetheless, if you use massed cells to catalyze some reactions or add some other special effects on massless pattern behaviors. Or you can hard limit population of massless states, by e.g. allow them to exist only while they are in contact with massed states M, then their population P is bounded 0≤P≤4M0 \le P \le 4M, at least after the first generation, as you can initialize a grid with 0 massed cells and 1000 massless non-vacumm cells, that will all die immediately.

How does the mass move?

  • Does the mass move at all? Yes e.g. SWAP01 / No e.g. CGOL confined to 1 mass cells.
  • Are there patterns where cell loses some mass and other cell gains some mass on arbitrary distance from each other, and no other change in mass of any states of cells happen? Yes e.g. copy left cell’s state / No e.g. SWAP01.
  • Are there ships and/or oscillators that change the mass numbers of their cells? Yes e.g. SWAP01 / No e.g. Abelian sand pile.
  • Can you modify the rule by adding another, completely inert cell type (‘wall’) without modifying behavior of patterns on toruses without this state? Or, inside in bulk of large nxn border made of wall, it should behave the same. All transitions that do not contain wall should be unchanged, and only transitions with wall added, while preserving mass conservation. Yes e.g. HPP / No e.g. copy left cell’s state.
    • Partial: you can place walls only e.g. above and below finite configuration, but not from left and right.

I think one of the critical qualities of mass conserving CAs is whether cells coordinate locally or globally. Both the property of being wallable and locality of mass change are trying to point at that, but there are differences. I guess.

Prototypical non-local coordination CA is the “copy left cell’s state” – they are non wallable, as stream of mass would get crashing against the wall and tail would not know there is no room and keep coming in. Coincidentally, this one would not be counted as mass conserving at all, given that definition above for infinite patterns with bijection.

The question the cell should ask, “would me switching from state C -> C’ change the global mass number?” and if it should rely on some behavior of other cells far away to get assurance that it will preserve the global mass number, I’d say it’s non local coordination.

There are exceptions, wallable CAs that can have non-local mass swap. For example 3 state 1d CA, where 1 mass gets transferred at a time. Then, long line of 1 mass slides left by 1 cell each generation – non local mass swap, effectively. But when it encounters a wall it “bunches up” to 2 mass cells and is fine.

Plausibly you should count the substates, and streaming vs state map steps separately to remove that non local mass swap condition.

What exactly are the conservation laws?

  • Are there additional spatial mass conservation laws? No e.g. HPP (there are additional momentum conservation laws) / Yes. e.g. SWAP01, mass is also conserved by rows.
  • Is there a period before mass stabilizes, or otherwise the mass is stable in all configs from first gen? No e.g. SWAP01 / Yes. e.g. SWAP01 but with additional state 3 that transitions to 0 regardless of neighborhood, but all cells nearby 3 become 1. – here some configs increase in mass from 0 gen to 1 gen, but then are necessarily stable.
  • Is mass property of states, or of patterns? States: SWAP01, HPP. Patterns: e.g. A cell is considered to have one mass chunk if it has at least one neighbor of B state.

Global mass conservation admits a lot of different ways to strengthen that condition, for example mass is also conserved by rows, or by diagonals or there are two kinds of mass and both are conserved separately. But I think, there should be a focus on at least having MC and only then looking at the particular type of it.

There can also be many relaxations, for example “mass can only be lost, but does so rarely”, this also leads to interesting kind of rule. Another is “mass is invariant on all transitions after the first”, this includes pretty trivial rules, where all cells die immediately and then mass = 0 and it stays, but also interesting ones with ships and oscillators.

Even looser example would be: SWAP01 but with additional state 3 that transitions to 0 if it has less than 4 of 3-state neighbors, otherwise stays. All non-3 cells nearby 3-state become 1. Else apply normal SWAP01 transitions. – no way to produce more of 3-state cells, and they decay with time. So, eventually mass stabilizes on all finite patterns, but there are finite patterns with arbitrarily large but finite mass increase.

This period before stabilization also makes it non trivial to search for “effectively” mass conserving rules, as they would fail naïve mass conservation checks on all small arrangements of cells.

Or, mass can be defined to belong to cells only in certain configurations, such as looking only at the cell’s state does not tell you for sure what amount of mass belongs to it.

Conflict resolution, methods to avoid mass duplication or vanishing.

  • Cell is split into substates that propose interaction with substate of the neighbors, importantly based only on the state of the cell, not its neighborhood. That makes it clear to other cells nearby that can observe that cell but not its whole neighborhood, what interactions are offered. Otherwise there would be uncertainty.
  • Cell gives off enough mass to cover all potential takers. Suppose it’s T - G - T, Takers on each side do not see each other, from their perspective it’s T - G - ?; If G offers 1 mass chunk, and T takes it, then it might be duplicated if there is another taker on the other side which takes it too. So, G needs to offer enough mass in a single go to assure any taker that there is enough for possible unseen takers, and mass wouldn’t be duplicated. Multiples of 4 in vN, multiples of 8 in Moore. (or less with complications, in Moore, as T placed orthogonally from G sees 4 out of 8 neighbors of G, and by allowing it only transfer orthogonally, G should give off >= 2 chunks)
  • Single incoming source and single outgoing direction. If cells are unambiguous about from what neighbor it takes and what neighbor gets an offer, then it’s easy to avoid conflicts. But then vacuum becomes anisotropic, or you’d need to have enough states to cover 4 incoming streams for vacuum cell in vN. Also it forces the state number too high, in vN it gets multiplied by 12, the number of arrows with source and destination. ==(NOTE can be maybe combined with 1d MC CA, with alternating 1d ca step and arrow change step? check this out)==
  • Streaming channel being under storage capacity. If cells have 4 neighbors, and at any generation can transfer 1 mass chunk to any neighbor, then you can avoid mass vanishing by e.g. setting the storage capacity to 5 or more, 0-1 being normal operation, 2 - 5 are overload states that are not open to receive any more mass.
  • Non intersecting templates. Generalization of “swap all 01 horizontal dominoes”. Template is a small region of the grid, its core is a subset of cells that will be altered if template matches that area, the witnesses are the cells that are not altered but are part of the template. Rule is made out of set of templates such that if any two templates overlaid with their cores intersecting, they differ in at least one place. So that at any given cell on the whole grid, only one template could possibly act on it. (should be cautious about final neighborhood size)
  • Phase coordination. If cells have “phase” such that to advance to phase N it needs to be at N-1, then it’s possible to communicate the cell’s expected neighborhood to its neighbors. E.g. phases 0 -> 1 -> 2, they can jump backwards to arbitrary phases, but forwards only to the next one. Then cell that has all neighbors in 0 phase can transition to “I currently have only 0-ph or 1-ph neighbors” – this can be used to communicate how much takers it sees for example. (hard to constrain it so much as to get strict-MC as you can artificially make unreachable arrangements of cells, but definitely possible to get MC after first generation.)
  • Just make neighborhood larger. If you use e.g. vN neighborhood of size 2, and mass transfers at half c, on one orthogonal step, then cells see the whole “neighborhoods” of cells they interact with. Expands map size and constrains the speed of mass transfer. Can be made isotropic.

The most difficult to conceptualize aspect of MC CAs is coordination of cells, for the purpose of preserving the mass. When one cell loses mass, it should be confident there is another cell to catch it. It should be confident there is no 2 cells ready to catch that mass. When cell gains mass, it should be confident that the cell that gave it, gave it only to this cell. Etc

As neighborhood is finite, a cell sees only part of the neighborhood the other cell sees. In vN the mutually observed area is just these two exact cells. In Moore it’s 3x2 rectangle, with those cells in the central column if they are orthogonally positioned and 2x2 square if diagonally.

That makes it challenging to orchestrate the exchange.

Partitioned state MC CAs

Basic concept explanation

Both Margolus and Morita CA are of this type. Also HPP realization into vN CA.

The basic idea is like that, what if you avoid conflicts and mass duplication altogether by splitting each state into several substates, then grouping several of such substates of nearby cells into enclosure and then permuting the tokens in those little enclosures? Would give mass conservation for free. Also reversibility if the whole map old_enclosure -> new_enclosure is a permutation.

Margolus gives each cell 4 substates. Update goes in two steps, make 4 enclosures over vertices of the square 4 substates in each. Update them. Then make enclosure out of all 4 substates in the central cell. Update it. Basically streaming and collision steps.

Morita does basically the same thing but with vN. Update goes in two steps, make 4 enclosures with 2 substates this time. Update them, by swapping them unconditionally. Then make enclosure out of all 4 substates in the central cell. Update it. Basically the same streaming and collision steps, but orthogonal this time.

Also that makes those rules at least quasi-isotropic or anisotropic.

I’m not explaining this like those people would, if you want their explanation go read their papers.

Design details and options

  • All particles are the same mass, 1 / Not, e.g. 1,2.
  • All particles are the same type / Not, e.g. mass 1 type 1 and mass 1 type 2 particles.
  • All slots can hold the same amount of mass / types, or Not.
  • Over streaming step, the substates just swap unconditionally / something other.
  • All substates are interacting with the fixed substate(s) of neighbor’s cell / Not, where with which ones depends on the cell state.
  • Are the particles unambiguously traceable? Not e.g. HPP on collisions / Yes e.g. SWAP01.

The core thing here is that all cells should agree on what exact enclosures they are part of, AND see those enclosures fully. So, it’s at most 2x2 squares in Moore and just 2 cell dominoes in vN.

You can do so much variations of this basic idea.

Both Margolus and Morita have static split, all cells do the same enclosure formation regardless of cells in their neighborhood. Both have 2 states of substate and 1 state of the particle. Both have enclosures changing based only on that enclosure itself, but nothing stops it from looking at the cells around it.

Really tricky part here is to avoid accidentally giving it larger neighborhood than you expected. Suppose cell has substate A interacting with one of above cell, and in next stage with substate of below cell. When you fold this into one update, cell below had interaction with a cell 2 hops above! That’s not good.

But the final full cell state -> new cell state map is always safe and free, as it does not include interaction with neighbors. You can add unlimited number of them, but they compose and you have equivalent of only one such map.

Some examples of MC CAs I made.

CA#1 “bootleg HPP”

gif example should be here

The idea was, for each cell to have 4 “airlocks”, one on each edge. Airlock can have a mover facing in, out, or no mover. (IN / OUT / NONE) So, there are 3^4 = 81 cell states.

The update goes in 2 stages: - Stream: On each edge between two cells, if there is one empty airlock and one with mover facing out, they get swapped and the mover changes state to IN. - Collide: Each cell transition to any of the other cells with the same amount of mass. As there are 81 states, it gives you a map, a vector of length 81 with elements 0..80

Those are very nice. Wallable, if you add another state that does no airlock swap. No stateful vacuum. Trivial to make quasi-isotropic. This specific version is all non-reversible, as streaming is non injective, but this can be modified.

Can be modified or simplified:

  • Remove in/out mover distinction, gives 2^4 = 16 states per cell. Equivalent to Morita’s PCAs. (Trivial to make reversible, by making state map into a permutation.)
  • Simplify streaming, for example just swap airlock contents unconditionally.
  • Each cell can consider its full pre-stream neighborhood in its collision step. That does not expand neighborhood.
  • Each edge swap can be more elaborate, only constraint is that mass(A, B) = mass(A’, B’). E.g. (OUT, OUT) -> (IN, IN).
  • Each edge swap can also take into account full state (pre stream) of those 2 cells between which it happens.
  • More states of the movers, e.g. (RED / BLUE) x (IN / OUT) or movers of mass 1 and mass 2.
  • There can be also multiple layers of airlocks, airlock get swapped with neighboring airlock of the same layer. But then collision step can happen across all layers, mapping full state of the cell to any other with that mass.
  • Massless or massed states can be added to cells, without attributing them to airlocks. E.g. a state that has all airlocks empty but is of 3 mass.
  • Each edge can be defined to have 2 airlocks, one pointed in and one out. Airlocks of cells connected (in to out) and (out to in), on each edge. Streaming step is defined to move movers from out airlocks to in airlocks, if they are empty, otherwise do nothing. (Can’t be made reversible by construction, but not completely forbidden from being emergently reversible.)
  • Each mover can be made to have “speed” by adding phase x speed state. If it’s in active phase, it can be streamed, if in passive forbidden from being streamed. For c/2 you just alternate them. For c/3 it’s active for one turn and then passive for 2 turns, etc.
  • Can be extended to Moore, if you add another 4 airlocks pointing to diagonal neighbors. Can be extended to n-d, trivially, if you add airlock per each face of the (hyper)cube.

CA#2 “generalized PCA”

gif example should be here

Each cell has slot A and slot B, each can hold 0 or 1 mass. So, total 4 states of the cell. Update goes in 3 stages:

  • State determines to what of 4 neighbors slot A points, to what B points, it can be the same neighbor.
  • If two cells have slot A pointing at each others, their A slot contents swap. Same with B.
  • Transition each cell state to some other state with the same mass according to state map.

This is just an abstraction, it can be expressed as a normal vN neighborhood simultaneous update rule, one stage.

link to JS page sim should be here

You can similarly construct PCA with 2K2^K states, KK is a number of slots/substates of 2 states. Or 2k1*3k2*4k3*...2^{k_1} * 3^{k_2} * 4^{k_3} * ... states, if you allow k1k_1 of slots with 2 states, k2k_2 of slots with 3 states, etc.

So, it’s a general way to make non-trivial MC and/or reversible CA of N states.

Superset of both Margolus and Morita’s CAs. Superset of previous model, so all ways to modify it apply here too.

Nice property of this family of rules is that it scales all the way down, to 2 state rule. 1 slot with mover of 1 state, this gives normal traffic rules.

Can be modified or simplified:

  • The movers can have more state or mass. 3 state MC CAs can be made.
  • Slots can point at any other slots, and swap happens if they point at each other.
  • When interaction of two slots happen, they can take into account full (pre-stream) states of their whole cells.
  • Final state map can take into account full (pre-stream) neighborhood of that cell.
  • Some states have more or less slots (e.g. state-1 has 4-2 slots, state-2 has 6 slot)
  • Trivial to extend to Moore or higher dimensions.
  • Non trivial to make reversible in general case. You can undo final state map. But then you can’t easily undo slot swap, as you don’t know to what neighbors they pointed before swap. But it’s easy to do if all of them point to the same ones like in the case with Margolus and Morita. Or if all single cell states have predecessors-by-streaming with only one type of slot pointer config. (pointed out by gpt-5.6-sol)

CA#3 “template CA”

gif example should be here

Motivation for this was to generalize “swap all 01 horizontal dominoes”. Loosely inspired by this paper https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08727 by Barbara Wolnik and others.

In Moore 2 cells that can see each other, can see also either 3x2 rectangle if they are placed orthogonally, or 2x2 square if diagonally. If those cells are of state-1 and state-0 they can decide to swap, based on this mutually seen neighborhood, as they are sure the other cell does complementary move.

Why only two cells? If you have some region, e.g. 3x3 with fixed cell values, 2 state, and define some population conserving transformation of cells within, then it could have been expressed with pairwise cell swaps. This frame restricts only the distance of the swap, to one Moore step. E.g. to express all 3x3 transformations you’d need range 2 Moore. Unclear how much is lost due to this, what other weird methods are there to achieve that without explicitly defining the swaps.

For range 1 Moore, this results in 4 templates, vertical and horizonal 3x2 rectangles with core in the center, and two 2x2 squares with different diagonals being the core. The rule is made from a set of such templates, where core sums to 1, as 1-1 swap or 0-0 swap does nothing.

Core marked C_x and mutually seen cells as T_x image should be here

This set of templates should be such as each cell on the whole grid has no ambiguity about which template’s core it is a part of. This is achieved by requiring that any two templates if overlaid with their cores intersecting, differ in at least one place. Overlap is made only by translating the templates, not rotation or reflection. To add rotations or reflections you need to add all the rotated/reflected versions of a template explicitly.

Why not allow templates where either they don’t overlap or overlap but prescribe the same action on the cell in consideration? Consider {01, 10} – those either differ or prescribe the same action on all overlaps. 01010 -> 10101 mass violation. They move two different mass units into central zero, but it only gets 1. And zeros on far sides get the same mass chunks moved in that moved in central one. Mass vanishing and duplication.

And it turns out you can enumerate the whole rule space pretty easily, the rules assembled from those 4 templates, takes like 20 minutes in unoptimized python. This construction method is very convenient, you can make that set of templates iteratively, by adding new templates that are compatible with all others and abort early if it fails to extend further.

CA#4 “2-pool sand pile”

gif example should be here

The goal for this one was to make a MC CA with actual isotropy while still displaying interesting behaviors.

Cell has input pool and output pool, each can contain 0..4 mass. So, 25 states in total.

If INP = 0 then in next generation add 1 to it for each neighbor with OUT = 4.

If OUT = 4 then in next generation subtract 1 from it for each neighbor with INP = 0.

If INP != 0 and OUT != 4 then transition to any other state with the same mass based on the whole vN neighborhood.

link to JS page sim should be here

Major problem here is that it’s impossible to evacuate 1 mass cells on the border of any pattern. You would need to fill the cell on boundary with vacuum to 4 and then it would dispense mass in all directions again, creating at least one 1 mass cell. Even if you prohibit the vacuum to take mass from some additional type of cell (e.g. add tail / head flag), the cells on the border will stay with the remainder, 1 mass, with no way to take it out.

So, there can be no ships, as they would bleed the mass on tail and sides.

There can be oscillators that move the mass, if you surround them with e.g. (1,1) cells, which deny incoming mass transfers and can be set to be stable. And the cells on the inside repeatedly exchange mass. (pointed out by Fable)

Unclear how to modify this to make it so that ships are possible, but strict MC and isotropy are preserved.

Can be modified or simplified:

  • Can be reduced in state number by sacrificing behavior complexity, for example by saying that mass in INP should be transferred to OUT as much as possible. Gives 9 states.
  • Cells after streaming can also transition to any other cell with the same mass based on (pre stream) full neighborhood.
  • More mass in pools, more pools, etc.

CA#5 “relaxed-MC 6 state”

gif example should be here

The goal for this one was to make a MC CA with actual isotropy while still displaying interesting behaviors. Succeeded on interesting behaviors and isotropy, but failed on strict MC.

Cells have phase state (0,1,2) and mass state (0,1) so 6 total. Written as (phase, mass). Range 1 vN neighborhood.

PRIORITY: if mass = 0, any phase, but no neighbors with mass =1, THEN transition to (0,0)

If self phase is 0 and mass = m, THEN transition to any of {(0,m), (1,m)} based on vN

if self phase is 1 and mass = m, THEN transition to (2,m) if only one neighbor has phase 1, otherwise to (0,m) or (1,m) based on vN

if self phase is 2 and mass = m, THEN copy the mass state of phase 2 cell nearby if there is only 1 of them, m -> new_m, THEN transition (0,new_m) or (1,new_m) based on vN

Set rules to have permutation symmetry. i.e. if 0 - 0,1,2,0 - 1 is a rule, then all permutation of neighborhood are assigned the same outcome e.g. 0 - 1,2,0,0 - 1.

To summarize, this family implements asynchronous proposal - acceptance coordination. Phase-1 transitions to Phase-2 only if it has 1 Phase-1 neighbor. Two phase-2 cells near each other swap their states. Phase structure makes it so that phase-2 cells can only have 1 phase-2 neighbor.

This is as close to strict-MC as it gets, while being relaxed-MC. Has non vacuum massless states, but necessarily bounded number of them relative to massed states.

Mass duplication or vanishing are completely forbidden after the first generation. But you can artificially make arrangements of cells (e.g. cross with (2,1) cell in center and 4 (2,0) cells nearby) that increase the mass going from gen-0 to gen-1, by bypassing the signaling and directly creating cells ready for exchange where it couldn’t arise naturally.

Has common ships and oscillators and is generally very nice rule family, somewhat visually similar to CGOL.

Can be modified or simplified:

  • More phases – would not fix artificially made mass violating pattern
  • Less phases – unclear how this would work, and if it works be probably degenerate
  • More mass, fancier exchange rules, etc.

CA#6 “the snakes”

gif example should be here

The goal of this one was to make patterns similar to that game where snake eats apples and grows. Simultaneous update, local and mass conserving CA version of it.

Each cell has arrow state, and mover state, and “remove” flag.

Arrow has a FROM and TO, one of the 4 sides, it can’t be the same side. Total 12 arrows.

image should be here

Mover can be either none, forward or backwards or both.

Arrow has 1 mass (or 0 if remove = 1), forward mover 1 mass, backwards mover 0 mass. overflow2, overflow3, overflow4 have 2,3,4 mass respectively.

12 x 2 x 2 x 2 = 96 states, plus vacuum, overflow2, overflow3, overflow4, total 100.

“backwards connecting” cell means, there is a cell on your FROM edge that points at you with its TO “forward connecting” cell means, there a is cell on your TO edge that points at you with its FROM

Streaming:

  • If cell has no forward mover, backwards connecting cell has one, THEN gain forward mover.

  • If cell has no backwards mover, forward connecting cell has one, THEN gain backwards mover.

  • If cell has forward mover, forward connecting cell has no such mover and remove = 0, THEN lose forward mover.

  • If cell has backwards mover, backwards connecting cell has no such mover, THEN lose backwards mover. Dealing with the ends of the chain:

  • If cell has forward connection, but no backwards connection, and backwards mover, THEN set remove flag = 1, lose backwards mover, gain forward mover.

  • If cell has remove flag = 1 and no forward mover, THEN transition to vacuum.

  • If cell has forward mover and vacuum is forward, THEN lose forward mover.

  • If vacuum has 1 arrow pointing at it with forward mover, THEN transition to some arrow with backwards connection to that arrow and backwards mover (free parameter here)

  • If vacuum has 2 or more arrows pointing at it with forward movers, THEN transition to corresponding overflow state.

This is rather long and complicated spec, but the basic idea is that there are 12 arrows with source and sink, each body cell has one. They pipe extra mass from tail to head and signal to disassemble the tail from head to tail. So, it crawls kinda by rolling (“caterpillar track”), taking tail cell and carrying it to the head over the body.
